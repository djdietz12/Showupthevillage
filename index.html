Why I’m showing up

In 2022, I was let go from my corporate job, and two weeks later we found out we were pregnant with our first child. I was lucky to still have my husband’s income, but I remember the panic and worry: how am I going to provide for this baby?

God ended up guiding me into real estate full-time, which doesn’t come with paid maternity leave unless you earn it in advance. Even after having my daughter, I remember the tug to get back out there so I could start generating business and providing.

I met a friend at Sweetgreen with my six-week-old daughter in tow. The woman behind the counter making my meal asked how old my daughter was, and when I told her, she said that was the same age as her daughter. I said, “Wow! Look at you, already up and at ’em!” She politely replied, “The bills don’t pay themselves.”

My heart was absolutely rocked. This mother was going through the same physical healing I was. Her daughter had the same bonding needs as mine. But she couldn’t be with her new baby, because she had to be on her feet for hours to collect a paycheck and pay the bills.

That conversation has stayed with me. Ever since, I’ve wanted to create a way to impact one mom at a time who doesn’t have the luxury of taking time away from work to bond with and care for her baby and herself.

The Village 5K aims to solve that. Our world needs more slowing down, more bonding with our children, and less pressure on our mothers.

By being part of the village, we get to step into the gap where a woman doesn’t have a village to help care for her and her baby. We have the blessing of helping her set a foundation for her child and her motherhood — with room to breathe, and her sole focus on loving her new baby.

Danielle Dietz

Show up

The call to action of the village is to show up. For each other. For a mother.

Our worlds are swirling with insatiable demands, and there are people in need all around us. It can feel daunting to know how to help. Showing up is the best way to start.

Show up for the loved ones in your life. Show up to support others. Don’t worry about what will come next — just show up, and the Lord will honor that.

The bigger picture

The United States has no national paid parental leave. The federal Family and Medical Leave Act protects up to 12 weeks of leave for eligible workers, but that leave is unpaid, and Florida has no state paid family leave program of its own.

27%

of U.S. civilian workers had access to paid family leave in March 2023 (Bureau of Labor Statistics).

16%

of workers in service jobs had it — the hourly roles many mothers hold (BLS, 2023).

Every dollar raised goes toward replacing a mom’s wages for 12 weeks after her baby arrives.
